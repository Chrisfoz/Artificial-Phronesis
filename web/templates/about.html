About Artificial Phronesis

What is this project?

Artificial Phronesis is a knowledge graph mapping the conceptual landscape at the intersection of wisdom, intelligence, and artificial intelligence. Using Neo4j and GraphRAG, we visualize how different academic disciplines—philosophy, psychology, and AI research—discuss overlapping but non-identical concepts.

Why a knowledge graph?

The field of AI wisdom research is fragmented. Psychologists study human wisdom through measurable traits like intellectual humility and perspective-taking. Philosophers debate the nature of phronesis (practical wisdom) and whether machines can possess it. AI researchers build self-improving systems with metacognitive capabilities but often lack grounding in wisdom science.

A knowledge graph lets us:

  • Map overlaps and gaps between different conceptualizations
  • Trace citations and intellectual lineages across disciplines
  • Identify research opportunities where concepts are under-connected
  • Query the landscape using GraphRAG to answer complex questions

Core Concepts

Wisdom

A meta-capacity combining cognitive, reflective, and affective traits toward balanced, pro-social judgment. Distinguished from intelligence by its focus on prudence over performance.

Phronesis

Aristotelian practical wisdom—the ability to deliberate well about what is good and expedient in particular contexts. Contrasted with theoretical wisdom (sophia) and technical skill (techne).

Intelligence

Cognitive ability focused on learning, reasoning, problem-solving, and abstraction. Traditionally measured by performance metrics (IQ tests).

Sapience

Higher-order wisdom; self-aware, reflective intelligence oriented toward good ends. From Latin "sapientia"—denoting wise intelligence marked by self-reflection and value-alignment.

Artificial Intelligence

Computational systems capable of tasks requiring human-like intelligence— pattern recognition, learning, reasoning. Traditionally focused on capability maximization.

Artificial Wisdom

Proposed extension of AI toward wisdom capacities: intellectual humility, perspective-taking, ethical reasoning, and concern for collective wellbeing rather than mere performance optimization.

Machine Phronesis

AI instantiation of phronesis—systems capable of context-sensitive ethical reasoning and prudent action-selection aligned with human values.

Computational Sapience

Sapient AI—systems with metacognitive monitoring, value-alignment mechanisms, open-ended self-improvement, and orientation toward collective flourishing.

Key Research Questions

  • How do definitions of "artificial wisdom" vary across disciplines?
  • Which human wisdom traits are already partially implemented in current AI architectures?
  • What capabilities are missing for machines to exhibit genuine phronesis?
  • How do we measure progress toward computational sapience?
  • Where are the under-theorized connections between concepts?

Methodology

  1. Literature curation: Collect foundational papers on wisdom (psychology), phronesis (philosophy), and artificial wisdom (AI)
  2. Entity extraction: Use LLMs to extract concepts, traits, and relationships from academic texts
  3. Knowledge graph construction: Load entities and relationships into Neo4j
  4. GraphRAG: Combine graph traversal with vector search to answer complex queries
  5. Visualization: Interactive graph exploration to discover patterns

Future Directions

This project aims to serve as a shared conceptual map for researchers working at the intersection of wisdom science and AI. Potential extensions include:

  • Temporal analysis of how concepts have evolved
  • Citation network analysis to identify key bridging works
  • Automated identification of research gaps
  • Integration with live academic databases (arXiv, Semantic Scholar)
  • Collaborative annotation by domain experts
